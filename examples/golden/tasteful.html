The Quiet Hour

Issue Nine A journal from a two-person letterpress shop Spring 2026

We printed eleven hundred sheets this season, and threw away two hundred and six of them. This issue is about the discards — what they cost, and what they taught us about the press.

Written between runs, in the hour after the shop closes and before the ink is washed off the rollers. Set in Fraunces and Source Serif.

01

The register drifts before you see it

A two-colour job holds register for maybe four hundred impressions before the packing compresses enough to matter. You do not notice at four hundred. You notice at six hundred, when the second colour has crept a hair north and the whole sheet reads slightly wrong without anybody being able to say why.

We now pull a proof every hundred and pin it to the wall in sequence. The wall is the only instrument we trust, because it shows drift as a shape rather than a number.

02

What the discards cost

Two hundred and six sheets is roughly a fifth of the run. Most shops would call that unacceptable and most shops would be right. We keep it because the alternative — proofing less and shipping sheets we are not sure about — costs more in the part of the ledger nobody writes down.

The discards go into the offcut box and come back out as postcards. They are the only thing in the shop that sells out every time.

03

On not buying the bigger press

We looked at a larger cylinder press in February. It would have doubled our sheet size and halved our make-ready time. We did not buy it, and the reason is boring: neither of us wanted to spend the next two years learning a new machine well enough to be careless on it.

The small press is slow in a way we have stopped resenting. It sets the pace of the shop, and the pace of the shop is most of why we opened one.
